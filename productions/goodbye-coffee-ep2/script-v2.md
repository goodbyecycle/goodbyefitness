# Goodbye Coffee — Ep. 2 · Script v2 (longer, more depth)

Narrator: the mug (first person). Target: ~2:30 with picture.

---

The bell over the door rang, and the cold came in with him.

Sterling.

It's been about a month since that day at the doctor's office.

A month doesn't sound like much.

It is when you're running out of them.

Overcoat buttoned to the collar. Newsboy cap. A cane now.

He didn't have the cane last month.

The coat used to fit him. Now it hangs off his shoulders like it belongs to a bigger man.

His face has gone thin. The gray's moved further into his beard.

Whatever's inside him isn't in a hurry anymore. It's just taking what it wants.

He waited in line like everyone else. He never asks for special treatment. Never has.

The girl behind the counter, dreadlocks tied up, smile like she meant it, filled me and slid me across.

"Careful. It's hot."

She watched him a second longer than usual. She noticed too.

He wrapped both hands around me anyway.

His fingers were cold. Colder than last Sunday. And they shook, just a little, until the heat found them.

He held on a little longer than he needed to.

The walk to the leather couch took a while.

Tap of the cane. A pause. Another tap.

He set me down. Lowered himself into the cushion like it might not hold him. Opened the journal. Ribbon still marking last Sunday's page.

He wrote one word at the top.

Devin.

Then he didn't write anything for a long time.

I found out later Devin was his brother.

Younger by two years. Used to follow Sterling everywhere, the way little brothers do.

Something happened between them at their father's funeral. I never learned what.

Sterling said it was something small. Something said in the wrong tone. Something not said at all.

He could've cleared it up right there, in the parking lot, in the rain.

He didn't.

So it sat.

And then it grew.

A missed Christmas. A phone call he let ring. A wedding he heard about secondhand.

Twelve years.

And here's the part that got me.

Sterling looked out that window and said, out loud, to nobody,

"I don't even remember what it was."

Twelve years with his brother, gone, over something he couldn't even name anymore.

And now the one thing he's got the least of is time.

His coffee went cold that morning. He never noticed.

Before he left, he turned to a fresh page and wrote something underneath his brother's name.

He pressed so hard the pen nearly went through the paper.

Then he closed the journal, took his cane, and walked back out into the cold.

So let me ask you.

That thing you didn't say six months ago.

The one that's still sitting there, growing.

Do you even remember what it was about?

Probably not.
