# Goodbye Coffee — Ep. 2 · Final script (approved)

The bell over the door rang, and the cold came in with him.

Sterling.

It's been a month since that day at the doctor's office.

A month doesn't sound like much.

It is when you're running out of them.

Overcoat buttoned to the collar. Newsboy cap. A cane now.

He didn't have the cane last month.

His coat hung loose. His face had grown thin.

He waited in line like everyone else.

The girl behind the counter filled me, smiled, and said, "Careful. It's hot."

He wrapped both hands around me.

His fingers were cold. And they shook.

He made his way to the leather couch, sat down, and opened his journal.

At the top of the page, he wrote one word.

Devin.

His younger brother.

Twelve years ago, something happened between them at their father's funeral.

Sterling said it was small. Something said in the wrong tone. Something that could've been settled in the parking lot.

But it wasn't.

One missed Christmas became two. Phone calls went unanswered. Birthdays passed. Then a wedding.

Twelve years.

Sterling stared out the window and whispered,

"I don't even remember what it was."

Imagine that.

Twelve years without your brother, and you can't even remember why.

And now, with so little time left, those twelve years were the one thing he couldn't get back.

He looked down at his journal.

He wrote something beneath his brother's name, pressing so hard the pen nearly tore through the paper.

His coffee went cold.

He never noticed.

Then he closed the journal, picked up his cane, and walked back into the cold.

So let me ask you something.

That argument you're still holding on to.

That person you haven't spoken to.

That thing you were so angry about six months ago.

Do you even remember what it was?

Probably not.
